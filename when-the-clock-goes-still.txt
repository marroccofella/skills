When T'Clock Goes Still.

Stop t'clock. Let t'gears lie down,
let mornin' lose its way.
You've gone where no bell can fetch you home,
and nowt knows what to say.

Pull t'curtains if it helps a bit;
let silence have its due.
But dun't tell me t'dark has won
while love remembers you.

Love were never soft as summer rain;
it were coal beneath t'grate,
a stubborn heat that held us fast
when frost came to t'gate.

So I'll not hush for death's convenience,
nor bow me head on cue.
I'll bear your name through wind and stone,
and make it thunder true.

Aye, stop t'clock and dim t'lamps;
let every bell fall dumb.
But rage, rage, rage while breath is mine;
t'dark won't have me numb.

And when t'fight has burned itself,
I'll sit where you once sat,
wi' one warm brew and one spare chair.

Grief's plain.
Love's price is that.
