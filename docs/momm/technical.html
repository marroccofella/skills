01 / A review protocol—not another foundation model

MOMM means Mixture of Model Modality. Here, “mixture” means orchestrating review routes. It is not mixture-of-experts training, a merged neural network, or evidence that every route uses a different model. “Modality” does not mean every reviewer accepts every media type: attachments depend on the verified adapter and account. See the supported modality boundaries.

Reviews fan out over the selected ready routes; they do not recursively call MOMM. The peers do not pass votes between themselves as a debate. Their separately requested reviews are rationalized for the governor. Duplicate grouping and agreement are attention aids, not ground truth. The useful diversity is in non-overlapping, valid observations, not the number of badges.

02 / Local control does not mean local inference

Tool allow/deny controls, temporary review directories and process cleanup reduce the attack surface. They do not establish perfect filesystem isolation, prevent every independently detached process, or prove that future CLI versions preserve the same behavior. Read the explicit privacy limits; do not treat provider login as permission to share a client’s code.

04 / Real development data—with denominators

The public 2026-09-04 snapshot contains 153 logged runs, 139 stored reports and 343 successful stored route responses. It records 302 applied, 251 rejected, 8 deferred and 9 historically labelled decisions. These are developer records, not a controlled customer trial.

Inspect the supervisor timeout fix and the rejected CRITICAL claim. The contrast is the benefit MOMM is designed for: discover a plausible defect, then test whether it is real. An applied row may be a suggestion, a duplicate or a modified fix—not one unique prevented production incident. We cannot infer bug-recall, false-positive rate, adoption or commercial return from these counts.

05 / Does stacking reviewers compound the benefit?

It can increase the chance that someone notices a defect. The size of that gain depends on detection ability and shared blind spots. It is not the same as reducing shipped defects: the governor must still recognise valid advice, fix it correctly and avoid regressions. Failed routes add no usable scrutiny.

P(all miss) = b + (1 − b) × (1 − p)n

n is the number of hypothetical reviewers; b is the shared-blind-spot subset: a fraction of defects invisible to every reviewer; p is each reviewer’s detection probability outside that subset, conditionally independent. b is not a general correlation coefficient. Common training data, the same wrong specification, overlapping model families and shared prompt weaknesses can all undermine independence.

Where do “orders of magnitude” come from? Under the idealised zero-blind-spot model, ten reviewers reduce all-miss probability from 50% to about 0.10%: 512× lower. Add the assumed 10% common blind spot and that comparison falls to about 5.45×; more reviewers cannot remove the common error floor. Neither figure is a measured MOMM result. Ten is a mathematical illustration, not a claim that the current default pool has ten external reviewers. Recursive stacking is explicitly forbidden.

06 / Overlap the waiting. Count all the work.

Choose reviewers by marginal value: additional confirmed defects and worthwhile suggestions per unit of total time and cost. A repeated confident error has no marginal value. The existing observed timing charts show route response times, not a serial-control experiment or a full productivity study.

For tiny edits, a review ensemble can cost more than it adds. Start with a small permitted reviewer set on representative tasks, then add a route only when its marginal contribution justifies the extra work. No reviewer count makes regulated, safety-critical or security-sensitive work automatically safe.

08 / How to test the benefit—not just market it

A credible evaluation would compare: (A) the current agent alone; (B) that agent plus equal-budget self-review; (C) MOMM with increasing external reviewer counts. Keep the base model, source snapshots and task distribution comparable. Randomise trial order and repeat runs; hold out tasks and use an independently defined oracle. Do not give MOMM extra budget and attribute the whole gain to architecture.

  1. Count unique verified defects after adjudication, with severity and false alarms. Do not use slug equality as defect identity.
  2. Include route refusals, timeouts, protocol rejection, total elapsed time, tokens/provider charges and governor verification time.
  3. Use task-level paired comparisons and confidence intervals; repeated reviewers of the same task are not independent task samples.
  4. Publish the task-selection method, sample sizes, missingness, model/CLI versions, settings and reproducible redacted artifacts with approval.

Customer uplift, cost savings and orders-of-magnitude quality claims stay unmeasured until such results exist. This is a proposed evaluation design; no trials are implied by publishing it.

09 / Related research—and the difference

Ensembling and multi-agent review are established research directions, not something MOMM can claim nobody else has attempted. MOMM’s specific combination is a portable skill, existing CLI account routes, a sole-writer governor, explicit dispositions and local evidence linkage. Superiority over another product needs a current, like-for-like evaluation.

  • Du et al., 2023: Improving Factuality and Reasoning through Multiagent Debate studies multi-round discussion on reasoning and factuality tasks. MOMM’s separately requested reviews and local reproduction gate are a different procedure.
  • Wang et al., 2024: Mixture-of-Agents passes previous-layer outputs to later agents and reports gains on response-evaluation benchmarks. Those benchmark scores do not quantify MOMM’s defect detection or developer productivity.
  • Zhang et al., 2025: Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate reports weaknesses against simple baselines across its evaluation and highlights model heterogeneity. More agents alone is not a reliable improvement guarantee.

These papers are related work, not independent validation, endorsement or measured results for MOMM. MOMM’s own public evidence and limitations are kept separate.
