# Search patterns

Read the patterns relevant to the requested booking. These are candidate constructions, not assumptions that they are cheaper.

## Flights and stopovers

For A → visit B → home C, compare:
- A→B plus B→C standalone against one A→B→C multi-city/stopover booking.
- Cash and partner awards on A→C as well as separate A→B and B→C. Through inventory can differ from component inventory; two separate awards are not equivalent to a through-ticket.
- All-premium against short leg Economy/long sleep leg Business; the reverse only if useful. Check each segment's fare, baggage and benefits. A global cabin selector or mixed-cabin headline is insufficient.
- Nearby C airports plus transport to the actual destination, including a genuinely usable included transfer.
- Adjacent allowed dates, same-price departure times and stopover lengths. Count local hotel nights and early/late check-in needs.
- Intended return/open-jaw when appropriate. Compare the total payable; never advertise half a return fare as a one-way ticket.

A more distant final destination can be cheaper because the supplier prices the market/itinerary, not just distance. Treat that as a hypothesis, not a mechanism proven by a fare screen. Use every booked segment as intended.

## Surface travel

Model stop/station/airport nodes and timed services. Test bus to an intermediate stop → short taxi to a rail station → train → final local transfer. Compare with staying aboard to a major interchange, direct taxi and all-public-transport.

Do not conflate retail parks, neighbouring shopping centres, terminals or station entrances. Verify the actual pedestrian route, crossings/stairs, distance and usable entrance. A same-complex address does not establish accessible proximity. Compare destination convenience with useful onward frequency: shortest taxi distance alone does not determine the best journey.

Use actual taxi quotes including minimum fare/surge when available. Directory estimates are leads. When a new quote changes the calculation, replan from the current stop; check nearby rail access before sending the traveller back to a distant hub.

## Accommodation, hire and other reservations

Apply the same goal-and-components logic: intended dates, exact property/product, quality and location requirements, total mandatory fees and usable inclusions. Test allowable dates/durations, room/category, meal/transfer bundles, cancellation terms, direct versus legitimate intermediary, eligible loyalty/credits and nearby locations with access costs.

For split stays/rentals, include relocation, luggage, duplicate fees, deposits/holds and handover time; check continuity. A nightly/daily headline can lose on the full stay. Rate labels or coupon mentions establish neither discount nor eligibility: compare the same product/date/conditions and verify qualification. Do not invent a trip, return, membership or extra stay to manufacture a lower headline.

## Search record

Keep a lightweight table rather than exhaustive tool logs:

| Candidate/hypothesis | Party size; constraints/date | Exact query or supplier | Result/status | Full party total + currency; unknowns | Next useful check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Evidence labels: lead; supplier quote; final review; reservation; ticket/confirmation. Separately record schedule, cabin, benefits and account evidence. Verification on one axis does not upgrade all the others.
